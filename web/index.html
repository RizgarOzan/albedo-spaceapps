Landing-site navigator

Click the map or enter a latitude/longitude (degrees, east-positive) inside the 20 km window. Each point gets its own terrain horizon (observer height selectable: 0 m matches the NASA maps, default 2 m; rays out to the window edge) and is scored over 2027 on Sun and Earth visibility. Sun and Earth directions are taken from the window centre; within 10 km that shifts them by at most about 0.3 degrees. The NASA columns are the published 18.6-year, 0 m maps, so the difference also contains the observer-height and time-span effects described on the Validation tab. Our Earth fraction is an upper bound: Earth stays only a few degrees above the horizon and terrain beyond the window is missing (some rim points read 100% where NASA shows about 50%). Ranking: mean of Sun and Earth fraction (the lower of ours and NASA's Earth value) minus a small penalty for the longest dark spell. Yellow dots: 48 px grid; blue: points you picked (*).

Horizon range: the three site rows are computed with rays out to 30 km on a 64 km LOLA window. Going from 10.24 to 30 km lowered the Shackleton Sun fraction from 88.7% to 86.0% and lengthened its longest dark spell from 102 h to 147 h. Points you click in the navigator still use the 20 km window (10 km rays), so their Sun hours are an upper bound for rim points with far ridges. Terrain beyond 30 km is not modelled.

Landing window planner

For every possible landing day of 2027, the 14 days after landing (about one lunar daylight period) are scored from this site's hourly Sun and Earth visibility. Score = min(Sun fraction, Earth fraction): the weaker of power and direct-to-Earth line of sight; no other weighting is applied. The top 5 are non-overlapping windows (start days at least 14 days apart), ties broken by the shorter longest dark spell. Late-December days are grey because 14 days would run past the end of the 2027 data. All values come from one year of data (2027) and carry the +-10 point uncertainty described on the Validation tab (docs/validation.md); read small differences between dates or sites as ties.

Top row: Sun fraction in the 14 days after landing on that day. Bottom row: Earth fraction. Brighter is more visible. Click a day to use it as the planned landing date.
