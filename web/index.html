Landing-site navigator

Click the map or type a latitude/longitude (degrees, east-positive) to score any point within about 10 km of the site. Each point gets its own terrain horizon and is ranked on how often it sees the Sun and the Earth in 2027. Yellow dots: grid points; blue: points you picked (*).

How the ranking works and where it is weak

Ranking: mean of Sun and Earth fraction (the lower of ours and NASA's Earth value) minus a small penalty for the longest dark spell. Observer height is selectable: 0 m matches the NASA maps, 2 m is the default. Sun and Earth directions are taken from the window centre; within 10 km that shifts them by at most about 0.3 degrees.

The NASA columns are the published 18.6-year, 0 m maps, so the difference also contains the observer-height and time-span effects described on the Validation tab. Our Earth fraction is an upper bound: Earth stays only a few degrees above the horizon and terrain beyond the window is missing (some rim points read 100% where NASA shows about 50%).

Why these numbers differ from the Explorer tab: points here only see terrain out to 10 km (20 km window), the Explorer uses 30 km. At the Shackleton site that is 88.7% Sun and a 102 h longest dark spell here against 86.0% and 147 h there. Trust the Explorer value for the three sites; for other points read Sun hours as an upper bound.

Landing window planner

Pick a landing day: each day of 2027 is scored on the 14 days that follow it (about one lunar daylight period). Score = min(Sun fraction, Earth fraction), the weaker of power and direct-to-Earth contact.

Details and limits

The top 5 are non-overlapping windows (start days at least 14 days apart), ties broken by the shorter longest dark spell. Late-December days are grey because 14 days would run past the end of the 2027 data. All values come from one year of data and carry the +-10 point uncertainty described on the Validation tab; read small differences between dates or sites as ties.

Top row: Sun fraction in the 14 days after landing on that day. Bottom row: Earth fraction. Brighter is more visible. Click a day to use it as the planned landing date.
