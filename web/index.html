102 hours of darkness. On Shackleton's rim in 2027 the Sun can stay below the horizon for 102 hours in a row (Earth for up to 354). This page shows when, for any point you pick. Horizon range: terrain is modelled out to 10 km only (the edge of the 20 km DEM window); anything farther away is not in the horizon.

Landing-site navigator

Click the map or enter a latitude/longitude (degrees, east-positive) inside the 20 km window. Each point gets its own terrain horizon (2 m observer, rays out to the window edge) and is scored over 2027 on Sun and Earth visibility. Sun and Earth directions are taken from the window centre; within 10 km that shifts them by at most about 0.3 degrees. The NASA columns are the published 18.6-year, 0 m maps, so the difference also contains the observer-height and time-span effects described on the Validation tab. Our Earth fraction is an upper bound: Earth stays only a few degrees above the horizon and terrain beyond the window is missing (some rim points read 100% where NASA shows about 50%). Ranking: mean of Sun and Earth fraction (the lower of ours and NASA's Earth value) minus a small penalty for the longest dark spell. Yellow dots: 48 px grid; blue: points you picked (*).
